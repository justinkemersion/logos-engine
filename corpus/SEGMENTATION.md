# Corpus segmentation doctrine

Logos Garden passages are **argumentative movements** — not database shards.

## Core principle

Passages should represent coherent **argumentative movements** or **dramatic exchanges**, not
isolated sentences.

A passage should:

- stand meaningfully on its own,
- fit comfortably within a single markdown file,
- preserve conversational and philosophical continuity.

Avoid:

- sentence fragmentation,
- exhaustive Stephanus slicing,
- excessively long uninterrupted discourse blocks.

## Why

| Too small (sentence-level) | Sweet spot (movement) | Too large (whole scene) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Semantic confetti | Intellectual fragments, essay-like units | Hard to read, review, and diff |
| Good for morphology research | Good for contemplative reading | Loses garden pacing |

**Greek blocks may be longer than translation blocks.** One file may contain several Greek
sentences unified under one conceptual movement. That is ideal.

## Terminology

Use **argumentative movement** internally when deciding boundaries:

- Plato’s dialogues are dramatic, but fundamentally conceptual.
- Boundaries follow **conceptual and conversational motion**, not pagination alone.

### Movement types (examples)

| Type | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Opening movement | Descent to Piraeus |
| Character speech | Cephalus on old age |
| Conceptual exchange | Simonides definition of justice |
| Argument escalation | Thrasymachus interrupts |
| Sustained argumentative movement | Justice as the advantage of the stronger |

## Review criterion

When reviewing generated garden markdown, ask:

> Does this feel like philosophy?

Not only:

> Is this technically accurate?

Technically accurate but spiritually dead prose is the main risk in Platonic translation.
Aim for calm, tension, irony, movement, and conversational life.
